# Source_data for Fig. 3: external validation

First row of each CSV = column headers (caption removed; captions below).

**Table S5** (`Source_data/Table_S5.csv`)

Table S5. Replication of discovery DEGs in external liver transcriptomic cohorts. Summary by cohort and histological feature for the primary analyses (GSE162694, GSE135251) and for the sensitivity analysis of GSE135251 adjusted for inferred sex. Columns give: discovery DEGs and the number measured externally; external DEGs; shared DEGs (FDR ≤ 0.05 in both); replication rate; genes in the same direction with binomial P; Spearman ρ among shared DEGs and genome-wide; the percentage of all common genes in the same direction; and discordant genes. External phenotypes: fibrosis F0 versus F1–F4; steatosis and inflammation versus NAS.

**Table S6** (`Source_data/Table_S6.csv`)

Table S6. Gene-level comparison of differentially expressed genes shared between the discovery cohort and external liver transcriptomic cohorts. Each row is a gene significant (Benjamini–Hochberg FDR ≤ 0.05) both in the discovery cohort (MAFALDA, n = 208; DESeq2 adjusted for age, BMI and sex; Table S1) and in an external cohort. Fibrosis was compared directly (F0 versus F1–F4); steatosis and lobular inflammation in the discovery cohort were compared with the NAFLD activity score (NAS, continuous) in the external cohorts. External cohorts were GSE162694 (DESeq2 adjusted for age and sex) and GSE135251 (primary analysis without covariates; sensitivity analysis adjusted for sex inferred from sex-chromosome gene expression). Rows: GSE162694, 46 fibrosis, 683 steatosis and 761 inflammation genes; GSE135251, 49, 853 and 969; GSE135251 sex-adjusted, 50, 868 and 981 (5,260 rows in total). Columns: Cohort, external cohort and analysis; Trait, discovery histological feature; Ensembl, Ensembl gene identifier; Symbol, HGNC gene symbol; Discovery log2FC, apeglm-shrunk log2 fold change for grade/stage ≥1 versus 0; Discovery FDR, Benjamini–Hochberg-adjusted P value in the discovery cohort; External log2FC, apeglm-shrunk log2 fold change for F1–F4 versus F0 (fibrosis) or per unit of NAS (steatosis and inflammation); External FDR, Benjamini–Hochberg-adjusted P value in the external cohort; Concordant, TRUE if the discovery and external log2 fold changes have the same sign. Summary statistics (replication rates, directional concordance and Spearman correlations) are given in Table S5.

**Table S7** (`Source_data/Table_S7.csv`)

Table S7. KEGG pathways enriched among fibrosis-associated DEGs in the discovery cohort and their replication in external cohorts. KEGG enrichment (clusterProfiler) was tested separately for up- and downregulated fibrosis DEGs in the discovery cohort (MAFALDA, n = 208; 65 up, 28 down; FDR ≤ 0.05; Table S1), with all tested genes as background. Pathways were retained at Benjamini–Hochberg FDR ≤ 0.10 with at least three genes (seven pathways: one down, six up). The same enrichment was performed on fibrosis DEGs (F0 versus F1–F4) of the same direction in each external cohort: GSE162694, GSE135251 and GSE135251 adjusted for inferred sex. A pathway was considered significant in an external cohort at FDR ≤ 0.10 with at least three genes. Columns: Direction, direction of the DEGs tested (Up or Down); ID and Description, KEGG pathway identifier and name; Category, KEGG top-level category; Discovery FDR and Discovery genes, enrichment FDR and number of DEGs in the pathway in the discovery cohort; for each external cohort, FDR, number of DEGs in the pathway, and whether the pathway was significant (TRUE/FALSE; blank FDR and gene count, pathway not enriched among that cohort’s DEGs); Replicated in, primary external cohorts (GSE162694, GSE135251) in which the pathway was significant. Six of seven discovery pathways were significant in at least one primary external cohort, and bladder cancer in both (Fig. 3d).

**Table S8** (`Source_data/Table_S8.csv`)

Table S8. Validation of hepatic lipid signatures in the ST000915 liver-biopsy cohort: summary. Discovery steatosis-associated hepatic lipids (MAFALDA, FDR ≤ 0.10; Table S3) were compared with the ST000915 liver lipidomics cohort (normal, n = 29, versus steatosis, n = 17; external models adjusted for age, BMI and sex). Of 854 discovery lipids, 187 were annotated, and 68 measurements (54 unique species) were present in ST000915. Columns give the number of discovery-significant lipids, external lipids significant at FDR ≤ 0.10 among those measured, the number measured externally, the numbers with the same and opposite direction of effect, and the percentage concordant. Feature-level results, including the class-specific concordance of triglycerides (48 of 50) and phosphatidylcholines (1 of 14), are given in Table S9.

**Table S9** (`Source_data/Table_S9.csv`)

Table S9. Validation of hepatic lipid signatures in the ST000915 liver-biopsy cohort: feature level. All 854 hepatic lipids associated with steatosis in the discovery cohort (MAFALDA; limma adjusted for age, sex and BMI; FDR ≤ 0.10; Table S3), matched by annotated lipid name to the ST000915 liver lipidomics cohort (normal, n = 29, versus steatosis, n = 17; adjusted for age, BMI and sex). 68 measurements (54 unique species) were present in ST000915; the remaining 786 (667 of them unannotated) could not be matched. Columns: Lipid main ID and Lipid Class; Discovery logFC and FDR (grade ≥1 versus 0); External logFC and FDR (steatosis versus normal); Direction (Up/Down) in each cohort; Measured externally (TRUE if matched in ST000915); Direction concordant (TRUE if the discovery and external effects have the same sign; blank if not measured). Of the 68 measured, 51 were concordant and 49 externally significant (FDR ≤ 0.10); concordance was 48 of 50 for triglycerides (47 significant) and 1 of 14 for phosphatidylcholines (summary in Table S8).
